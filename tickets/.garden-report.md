## Open correctness bug, now at the head of the queue

`fix/framed-read-false-truncation` (`repro: verified`, filed by the `15.32` review). **A frame the
remote actually wrote can be reported to the caller as truncated.** `readFramed` concludes "the
remote finished writing" while the reply bytes are still in flight and raises
`FrameTruncationError` on a healthy connection carrying a complete reply; the trigger is how many
microtasks elapse inside the inbound handler before its first write. The site is
`remoteFinishedWriting` plus the 20 ms `EOF_POLL_MS` loop in `protocols.ts` — the poll restored by
`15.12-halfclose-fix`, which turns out to overshoot. `readFramed`'s own doc block asserts the
invariant it violates.

Effect on a live ring: a good reply from a healthy peer is discarded and booked as `decode-error`,
decaying that peer's relevance. It is **not** in `v1.0.0-beta.1` (which predates framing entirely),
but it is on `main`, which optimystic tracks via its `portal:` dependency.

The runner was restarted as `--stages fix,review,implement,plan:15.5` so this leads rather than
queueing behind five cleanup and test tickets. That alone was **not** enough: the fix stage emitted
its implement ticket *unnumbered*, which starved it behind every numbered `15.3xxx` sibling one
stage later (see the trap below — this is its second occurrence, and the first where the runner
itself produced the unnumbered name). Renumbered to `15.0-` so it leads the implement queue.

## The false-truncation bug is fixed and verified

`framed-read-false-truncation` closed at **`deb20ef`, 991 passing / 0 failing**, after three review
passes. The fix is structural rather than a retune: the 20 ms `EOF_POLL_MS` poll and
`remoteFinishedWriting` are **deleted outright**. `readFramed` now has two implementations behind
one signature, dispatched on `isMessageStream` — a real libp2p stream reads its prefix and body
through `@libp2p/utils`'s `byteStream`, which rejects with `UnexpectedEOFError` the instant the
stream ends short of the bytes it was promised, so the frame is read at the same layer that owns
the stream's EOF state (no timer, no poll); a plain async iterable ends on iterator EOF alone and
raises `FrameTruncationError`. Both names are matched by `isFrameTruncationError`, so either
surfaces as `decode-error`.

The invariant is now pinned from both sides: `test/rpc.stream-errors.spec.ts` bounds an
ended-stream read at an order of magnitude inside the RPC budget, and a new
`test/rpc.two-node-framed.spec.ts` drives real framed replies between two nodes across a spread of
reply timings (immediate, several microtask depths, one deferred past a macrotask) and requires
that **a reply really written is never reported cut off**.

## A scheduling trap worth knowing about

`15.2b-rpc-sender-migration` sat in `implement/` across four live re-ranks while the tree stayed
red at typecheck, and it was not a runner bug. **A filename like `15.2b-` carries no valid numeric
sequence prefix** — the trailing letter breaks the parse — so tess treats the ticket as
*unnumbered*, and unnumbered tickets sort after every numbered one in the same stage. Its siblings
`15.31`/`15.32`/`15.33` parse fine, so they outranked it on every pass. Same trap applies to the
`15.2a1` / `15.2a3` style names.

It was renamed to `15.2-` (numeric, and correctly ahead of the 15.3x chain) and picked up
immediately.

**It has now happened twice, by two different routes**, so treat it as a standing hazard rather
than a one-off: a letter-suffixed split (`15.2b`), and a stage transition that emits its output
ticket with *no* prefix at all (`fix/framed-read-false-truncation` → `implement/` at
`Sequence: --`). The second is the worse of the two, because stage-order priority cannot rescue it
— putting `fix` first in `--stages` gets the ticket *through* the fix stage, and then it starves in
`implement/` anyway.

Two rules fall out: **splits should suffix with a decimal (`15.21`), never a letter**, and **a
ticket emitted into a stage that already holds numbered tickets needs a number**. Symptom to watch
for: a ticket sitting in a stage across several transitions while the runner works higher-numbered
siblings in that same stage. `Sequence: --` in the runner's transition banner is the direct tell.

**A third route, and this one is not a naming problem at all.** `review/15.0-framed-read-false-truncation`
sat unpicked for an hour while the runner ground through `implement/15.3xxx` — even though `review`
comes *before* `implement` in `--stages` and 15.0 is the lower sequence. The cause is the `live`
strategy's **chain affinity**: it prefers finishing the chain it is already in, and that preference
outranks both stage order and sequence. It is not a bug — it is what keeps a split ticket's slices
together — but it means a ticket parked mid-chain can wait indefinitely when the active chain keeps
spawning siblings (`15.32555` → `15.32556` → …), which this one did.

Neither renaming nor reordering `--stages` fixes that. What works: stop the runner at a clean tree
and start one scoped to the stage you want drained (`--stages review`), then restore the broad
invocation. Worth doing when the parked ticket is more important than the chain in flight — here a
verified correctness fix whose only outstanding step was running the suite.

## Note on the runner's budget

`--token-budget 120000` repeatedly stalls an agent mid-discovery: it writes its findings into the
ticket and exits without editing, and the next run does the edit. That costs roughly double on the
stalled runs. **Left in place deliberately.** The stalls are what force these tickets to
decompose, the resulting per-file handoff notes are accurate, and one of those investigation-only
runs is what found the half-close regression instead of shipping it. A larger budget would more
likely land a big ticket as one half-finished edit than as clean slices.
