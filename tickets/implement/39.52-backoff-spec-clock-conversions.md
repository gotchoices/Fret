`setBackoffOf`) arrives with `backoff-test-helper`; read that ticket's *Verified facts about HEAD*

## `failure-recovery.spec.ts`

Sites: `:74-80` (local `backoffMap()` accessor plus an `expireWindow`-style helper that writes
`until: Date.now() - 1`), `:167`, `:241`, `:247`.

The local accessor and the hand-written expired `until` both go. Install a `ProbeBackoff` with a
controllable clock through `setBackoffOf` in the rig, and expire a window by **advancing that
clock** rather than writing `until`.

Advance by at most `baseMs * maxFactor + 1` (32 s), which is well under `retainMs` (300 s), so the
escalation survives. Advancing past retention would silently reset the factor to 1 and make the
"window doubles per confirmed failure" assertion at `:247` pass for the wrong reason.

Assertions become `factor(id) === 0` for "no backoff" and `factor(id) === 1|2` for the escalation.

## Watch for

**`factor(id) === 0` is the honest replacement for `map.get(id) === undefined`.** A retained but
window-closed entry reports `factor > 0`, so the two differ exactly where the retention rule
matters — which is the point of the extraction, and why every "no backoff recorded" assertion
should read `factor === 0` rather than probing for absence.

**Don't reintroduce a second cast** to reach `ProbeBackoff` internals. If a spec wants something
the public surface does not expose, raise it in the handoff rather than casting around it.
