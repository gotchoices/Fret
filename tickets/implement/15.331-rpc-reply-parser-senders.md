First of two tickets split out of `implement/15.33-rpc-reply-parsers-and-caps`, which hit the
runner's token budget during investigation — **nothing had landed**; this is a size split, not a
scope change. This ticket does the **sender/reply-parser** half. `15.332-rpc-snapshot-cap-single-point`
does the service wiring, the dead-slice removal and the docs, and depends on this one.

## Already landed — do not re-implement

- `parsePingResponse`, `parseNearAnchor`, `parseMaybeActReply`, `makeSnapshotParser(caps)` all
  exist in `src/rpc/validate.ts` from `15.31`. This ticket only wires them.
- `rpcRequest` (`src/rpc/request.ts`) owns the whole outbound open/write/read/release sequence and
  every sender is already a thin wrapper over it. Its `decode` callback is where a reply validator
  belongs.

## Verified facts about the seam (read the code; these are current)

- `rpcRequest`'s decode phase is `decodeReply` in `src/rpc/request.ts`. It parses the body once for
  the busy check, then calls `decode` inside a `try`; a **throw** becomes `decode-error` via
  `decodeFailure` (or `cancelled` if the caller's signal already aborted). A busy reply is
  short-circuited *before* `decode` runs, so a reply parser never sees one.
- **There is no `undefined` check anywhere in that path.** `decode`'s signature is
  `(bytes: Uint8Array) => T | Promise<T>`.
- Therefore passing a `Parser<T>` straight in as `decode` yields `{ kind: 'ok', value: undefined }`
  — exactly the "an `ok` carrying `undefined` dressed as the reply" failure `rpcRequest`'s two
  overloads exist to prevent. It also type-checks silently (`T` infers as `PingReply | undefined`),
  so nothing catches it at compile time and the caller reads `.ok` off `undefined` at runtime.
- `decode-error` is proof of life, never a contact strike (`noteRpcFailure`), so a reply rejection
  is the right classification.

## Decision to make (the original ticket left it open; pick one and say which in the handoff)

- **Recommended: an adapter, shared by the senders.** Add one named helper beside the parsers —
  e.g. `parseOrThrow<T>(parse: Parser<T>, msg: unknown): T` in `src/rpc/validate.ts` — that throws
  a named error when the parser returns `undefined`. Each sender's decode becomes
  `async (b) => parseOrThrow(parsePingResponse, await decodeJson(b))`. Leaves `rpcRequest`
  untouched and keeps `undefined` a legal reply value for any future consumer of the exported
  helper. Give the thrown error a stable `name` so it is greppable and so a test can assert on
  identity rather than message text (the house rule the `isFrameTruncationError` /
  `isPayloadTooLargeError` pair already follows).
- The alternative — `rpcRequest` treating a returned `undefined` as `decode-error` — is simpler at
  the call sites but makes `undefined` unreturnable as a legitimate reply for every consumer of a
  publicly exported helper. Weigh before choosing.

## `fetchNeighbors` signature

Today: `fetchNeighbors(node, peerIdOrStr, protocol = PROTOCOL_NEIGHBORS, opts: { signal?, timeoutMs? })`.

It must take the parser (or the caps) from its caller. Three test call sites pass no parser
(`test/ring-membership.spec.ts:871`, `test/rpc.protocols.spec.ts:632`, `test/rpc.stream-errors.spec.ts`),
so an added `parse` on the options bag needs a default. `registerNeighbors` already set the
precedent for exactly this problem — its `snapshotParser` parameter defaults to
`makeSnapshotParser({successors: Infinity, predecessors: Infinity, sample: Infinity})`, i.e.
"validate the shape, truncate nothing", with a `NOTE:` saying the default is reachable only from
tests. Follow that precedent and its NOTE rather than inventing a second convention; the service
always supplies real caps (that wiring is `15.332`).

## Confirm before trusting the reply caps

`parseNearAnchor` caps `anchors` at 8 and `cohort_hint` at 16. Check those against `pickAnchors`
(≤ 2) and the cohort-hint producer (≤ 8) so a cap provably cannot refuse our own legal output.

## Key tests to expect

Extend `test/rpc.codec-properties.spec.ts`:

- The round-trip property extended to the **wired** path: a legal reply produced by our own encoder
  survives its sender's `decode` — `rpcRequest` returns `ok`, never `decode-error`. Cover all three
  senders.
- A malformed reply (fuzzed field types) yields `decode-error` — never a throw out of the sender,
  and never an `ok` carrying a half-parsed value or `undefined`. **This test is the point of the
  adapter decision above**: written against a parser wired in raw it fails; written against a
  thrown validator it would pass vacuously, so assert on `out.kind === 'ok' ? out.value : …` shape
  rather than merely on `kind`.
