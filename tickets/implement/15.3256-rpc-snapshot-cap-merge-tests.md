## Where the work being counted lives (verified against the current tree)

`onAnnounce` receives an **already-parsed** snapshot, so the parse + hash + upsert loop this
ticket counts is `FretService.mergeAnnounceSnapshot` (`src/service/fret-service.ts:1771`), not
`registerNeighbors`. Its caps come from `FretService.mergeSnapshotCaps()` (`:1751`):

| profile | successors | predecessors | sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| core | 16 | 16 | 8 |
| edge | 8 | 8 | 6 |

The same object is handed to `makeSnapshotParser` at the single registration site
(`fret-service.ts:1033`, with a comment saying "do not inline the numbers"), and the parser
truncates before the merge loop ever runs. That the parser's caps and the merge loop's `slice`
are the same numbers is exactly what this ticket pins.

### What counts as one unit of work, and why `store.upsert` is the right counter

`mergeAnnounceSnapshot` calls `this.store.upsert` in exactly three places: once for `from`
(`:1782`), once per successor/predecessor id after `hashPeerId` (`:1804`), once per `sample`
entry after `base64urlToCoord` (`:1815`). So the count is `1 + succ + pred + sample`.

`applyTouch` (`:497`) opens with `this.store.getById(id) ?? this.store.upsert(id, coord)` — the
entry always exists by then because the merge loop just upserted it, so `applyTouch` does **not**
double-count. Verified by reading, not measured; if a count comes out doubled, that assumption is
where to look first.

### Why the unstarted-service rig stays quiet

- `enforceCapacity` (`:462`) early-returns while `store.size() <= capacity` (2048), so no
  eviction noise perturbs the count.
- `announceToNewPeers` (`:1726`) filters targets by `hasAddresses(id)`; with nothing in the
  peerStore the target list is empty and it returns before any dial. So the detached announce
  is a no-op on an unstarted service — no open handles for the mocha exit watchdog to catch.

## Rig notes

- The model is the `handleMaybeAct validator consequences` describe (spec ~1259): `createMemNode()`,
  `await node.start()`, `new CoreFretService(node, { profile, networkName: NETWORK })`, service left
  **unstarted**, `await stopAll([node])` in `afterEach`. Reach privates through a `DrivableService`
  interface cast (that describe declares its own; declare a new one for these tests rather than
  widening that one).
- Privates needed: `mergeAnnounceSnapshot(from, snap)` and `mergeSnapshotCaps()`. Awaiting
  `mergeAnnounceSnapshot` directly is what makes the count deterministic — `handleAnnounce`
  `detach`es it.
- `peerIdStr(seed)` (spec ~52) mints parseable base58btc peer ids; seeds must stay under 256 to
  stay distinct. `from` must be one of these — `mergeAnnounceSnapshot` calls `peerIdFromString(from)`.
- `makeSnapshotParser` and `coordToBase64url` are already imported by the spec (lines 8, 13).
- `wrongWidthCoord(n)` and `sampleCoord(byte)` already exist but are **scoped inside** the
  `announce snapshot field matrix` describe (~836). Hoist them to module scope rather than
  re-declaring — a second copy of the wrong-width-coordinate rule is the drift these tests exist
  to catch.
- Count by wrapping `svc.getStore().upsert` on the instance, recording the ids passed.

## TODO

- Add a service-tier describe to `test/rpc.handler-fuzz.spec.ts`, table-driven over both profiles.
- **Over-cap snapshot case.** Build a snapshot with far more ids than any cap (e.g. 40 successors,
  40 predecessors, 20 sample). Assert, by the `upsert` counter and the recorded ids:
  - the merge loop upserted exactly `1 + caps.successors + caps.predecessors + caps.sample` times,
  - the ids merged are the **first** N of each list, in order, and ids past the cap are absent
    from the store,
  - `mergeSnapshotCaps()` returns the hard-coded numbers in the table above — pinned literally, so
    the expectation is not derived from the thing under test,
  - feeding the same raw body through `makeSnapshotParser(caps)` first yields the *same* upsert
    count as merging the untruncated snapshot. That equality is the ticket's claim: an over-long
    list costs the receiver no more than its own stated limit either way.
- **Short-`coord` sample case.** Both halves, per the skip-and-log-per-entry rule:
  - through `makeSnapshotParser`: the bad entry is absent from the parsed snapshot and the good
    entries survive (so it never reaches `onAnnounce`),
  - straight into `mergeAnnounceSnapshot` (parser bypassed, which is the arm the merge loop's own
    try/catch at `:1817` exists for): only that entry is skipped, every other id is upserted, and
    the merge does not throw or reject the message.
