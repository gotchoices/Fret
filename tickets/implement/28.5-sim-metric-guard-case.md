## Measured inputs

**Relevance spread — measured 2026-08-21, phase A of `sim-relevance-scoring`.** The
sim-local `alpha` fallback was **not needed**: the bonus is not clamp-degenerate. One peer's
bounded store (largest store on the converged ring, `capacity: 32`, seed 4242, 4 s convergence):

| case | store | bonus p10 / p50 / p90 | at `sMax` 1.8 | at `sMin` 0.7 | relevance p10 / p50 / p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n=200 cap=32       |  32 | 1.175 / 1.196 / 1.212 | 1/32 (3.1%)  | 0 | 0.939 / 0.950 / 0.968 |
| n=1000 edge cap=32 |  32 | 1.174 / 1.201 / 1.217 | 1/32 (3.1%)  | 0 | 0.721 / 0.875 / 0.897 |
| n=200 unbounded    | 185 | 1.185 / 1.185 / 1.206 | 1/185 (0.5%) | 0 | 0.932 / 0.949 / 0.957 |

The single at-`sMax` entry in each case is the distance-0 self entry. **But the spread is far
narrower than the ticket predicted, and for a reason that is about the metric, not the KDE:**
every non-self entry lands in the single x-bin [0.9, 1.0). `normalizedLogDistance` is
logarithmic over a 256-bit ring, so uniformly-placed peers concentrate at x ≈ 0.9–1.0
regardless of how many there are — occupancy at n=200 cap=32 was
`[0.025, 0.009, 0.001, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.003, 0.057, 0.396, 0.927]` against centers
0.042…0.958. So there is a gradient (the low-x centers are genuinely sparse and would pay
≈ 1.8), but **no peer ever sits there to collect it**: the whole live population spans
≈ 4% of the bonus range (1.17–1.22). Eviction therefore still ranks almost entirely on
the base score, not on sparsity. Treat "eviction now prefers far peers and keeps a near/mid
spine" as **not established** — see *Open question* below.

4 s convergence, seeds 1 / 4242 / 99 / 20260820, `churnRatePerSec: 0`. Every store saturates at
the cap, so knowledge fraction is exactly `capacity / n`. Per-cap min/max across the four seeds:

| cap | n=200 know | n=200 succ | n=200 p90 | n=1000 all-edge know | n=1000 succ | n=1000 p90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 |  8.5% | 100%     | 11–12 | 1.7% | 40–52%   | 19–21 |
| 20 | 10.0% | 100%     |  6–7  | 2.0% | 89–97%   | 15–19 |
| 24 | 12.0% | 100%     |  4–5  | 2.4% | 99–100%  | 12–15 |
| 28 | 14.0% | 100%     |  3–4  | 2.8% | 98–100%  |  9–12 |
| 32 | 16.0% | 100%     |  3    | 3.2% | 100%     |  7–8  |
| 40 | 20.0% | 100%     |  2–3  | 4.0% | 100%     |  6–7  |
| 48 | 24.0% | 100%     |  2–3  | 4.8% | 100%     |  4–6  |

- **`capacity`: 24, on the n=1000 all-edge ring** (`profileMix: { edge: 1, core: 0 }`) — smallest
  value clearing all three criteria (knowledge in low single-digit percent, p90 ≥ 3, success ≥ 95%)
  on all four seeds. Measured there: knowledge 2.4%, success 99–100%, p90 12–15, max 17–23.
  The knowledge criterion is what selects the ring: at n=200 the store saturates at `capacity / 200`,
  so knowledge never falls below 8.5% for any capacity at or above the 17-id protection floor.
- **The n=200 dense ring is still available if the guard wants a cheap case**, at the price of the
  knowledge criterion: capacity 28 is the smallest giving p90 ≥ 3 on all four seeds (3–4) at 100%
  success, and capacity 32 is the tightest (p90 exactly 3 on every seed).
- **Bite headroom at the chosen point is real and was measured.** Success at n=1000 falls off a
  cliff just below the chosen capacity — 89% at cap 20 (seed 20260820), 40–52% at cap 17 — so the
  route attempt budget is near-binding at cap 24 and a worse metric has somewhere to fail. Contrast
  the unbounded stores this spec uses today, where a substituted metric moved neither number.
- **Provenance caveat (added at review).** The retained raw output
  (`tickets/.logs/28-sim-capacity-sweep.log`) covers caps 20–48 and reproduces every row above
  exactly. The **cap-17 row is not in it** — it came from a separate earlier invocation whose
  output was not retained. Treat cap 17 as indicative only; the cliff argument does not depend on
  it, since the cap-20 drop to 89% success is in the retained log.
- **Guard-margin warning (added at review).** At cap 24 the n=1000 success rate is 99–100%, only
  4 points above the 95% floor, and cap 20 already dips to 89% on one seed. A shipped assertion of
  `success >= 95%` at cap 24 is therefore one parameter-shift (churn, route count, seed set) from
  flaking. Cap 28 (success 98–100%, p90 9–12) and cap 32 (100%, p90 7–8) both sit far above the

## Open question this ticket inherits

The guard here wants stores that are *sparse and finger-shaped*. Phase A shows a capacity bound
alone gives sparse-but-uniform: a bounded store is a near-uniform sample of the same far-x
population, because that is where the log metric puts everyone. If the swept capacity turns out
to give the p90 ≥ 3 the guard needs, that is enough regardless — the guard measures hop
count, not store shape. If it does not, the missing ingredient is a distance-shaped admission or
eviction bias, not a smaller cap, and that is a new plan-stage question rather than a knob to
turn here.

## Why

`test/simulation.routing.spec.ts` guards routing direction (inverted preference moves p90 from 2
to ~19) but not metric *quality*: substituting `clockwiseDistance` or XOR for `minDistance`
moves neither threshold, because dense unbounded stores let greedy routing land in 1–2 hops
under any roughly-monotone metric. Measured by the plan run (100 routes per case, seeds
1 / 4242 / 99 / 20260820):

| selector distance function          | success  | p90 hops  | max hops |
|-------------------------------------|----------|-----------|----------|
| `minDistance` (shipped)             | 95–100%  | 2         | 3        |
| preference inverted (farthest wins) | 99%      | 17–20     | 22       |
| `clockwiseDistance` substituted     | 100%     | 2         | 3        |
| XOR distance substituted            | 94–98%   | 2–3       | 3        |

With sparse capacity-bounded stores (the prereq), a substituted metric has headroom to measure
worse.

## The guard

New case inside **`test/simulation.routing.spec.ts`** — not a new spec file: that spec's
doc-block states the limit being closed, so the case and the retracted caveat are edited in one
place.

- capacity-bounded sparse ring using the measured `capacity`; assert success ≥ 95% and
  p90 hops ≥ 3 under the shipped `minDistance`
- the case must **bite**: demonstrate (as the inverted-preference measurement above did) that
  substituting `clockwiseDistance` or XOR moves the number past the threshold. If it does not
  bite, the guard is not written yet — report the measured substituted-metric numbers rather
  than shipping a threshold nothing can fail.

### The stated assertions cannot bite. Fix the guard direction first.

*The guard* says: "assert success >= 95% and p90 hops >= 3 under the shipped `minDistance`", and
separately "the case must bite: substituting `clockwiseDistance` or XOR moves the number past the
threshold". Those two sentences are in tension:

- **`p90 >= 3` is a lower bound.** A worse metric makes routes *longer*, so it moves p90 **up**,
  away from that bound. No substituted metric can ever fail `p90 >= 3`. That assertion is a
  *headroom* claim — "this case is genuinely multi-hop, so a metric has room to be worse" — the
  exact role `knowledgeFraction < 0.5` already plays in the existing n=1000 case. Keep it, but do
  not count it as the guard.
- **The biting assertion therefore has to be one of:** a p90 **upper** bound (`p90 <= X`, with X
  chosen inside the measured gap between the shipped metric and the substituted ones), or the
  success floor (`success >= 95%`) if a substituted metric drops success below it, or both.
- This reconciles the *Guard-margin warning* above, which already anticipated it in passing
  ("unless it also asserts a p90 *upper* bound that only cap 24 satisfies"). That sentence is the
  live design question, not an aside: **the upper bound is the guard**, so the cap choice must be
  made against the substituted-metric numbers, which nobody has measured yet at any capacity.

So the measurement the next run owes is not "does the shipped metric clear p90 >= 3" (the sweep
already answered that: 9–12 at cap 28, 7–8 at cap 32). It is **"what do the substituted metrics
measure at that same cap"** — that number is what decides both the upper bound and the capacity.

Note the existing `MAX_P90_HOPS` is 6 and every current case asserts p90 at most 6; this case's
upper bound will be a second, case-local constant well above it (shipped p90 is 7–12 here), which
is fine but must be named and commented as its own thing, not a loosening of the shared one.
