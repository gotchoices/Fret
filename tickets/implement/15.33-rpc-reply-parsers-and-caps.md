Last of three tickets split out of `implement/15.3-rpc-json-handler-seam` (the original hit the
runner's token budget before any code was written — **nothing had landed**; this is a size split).
`15.31-rpc-validators-module` wrote the parsers, `15.32-rpc-json-handler-seam` put them on the
inbound path. This one puts them on the **outbound reply** path and makes the snapshot caps one
number. `15.4-rpc-byte-cap-tightening` depends on this ticket.

## Already landed — do not re-implement

- `rpcRequest` (`src/rpc/request.ts`) is the single owner of the outbound open/write/read/release
  sequence. Its `decode` callback is exactly where a reply validator belongs: a throw from `decode`
  is already classified `decode-error` (proof of life, never a contact strike) by `decodeFailure`.
  A busy reply is tested *before* `decode` runs (`decodeReply`), so a reply validator never sees one.
- All four senders already go through it: `fetchNeighbors` / `announceNeighbors`
  (`src/rpc/neighbors.ts`), `sendPing` (`src/rpc/ping.ts:83`), `sendMaybeAct`
  (`src/rpc/maybe-act.ts:88`), `sendLeave` (`src/rpc/leave.ts`). Three of them pass a `decode` that
  is a bare `decodeJson<T>` cast; `sendPing`'s coerces with `Boolean(r.ok)`.
- `mergeSnapshotCaps()` (`src/service/fret-service.ts:1700`) is the single source of truth for the
  per-message merge caps (Core 16/16/8, Edge 8/8/6), shared by `fetchAndMergeSnapshot` and
  `mergeAnnounceSnapshot`. Reuse it; do not introduce a second copy of those numbers.
- `parsePingResponse`, `parseNearAnchor`, `parseMaybeActReply`, `makeSnapshotParser(caps)` all exist
  from `15.31` — this ticket only wires them.

## Design

### Reply shapes get parsers too

The plan left this open; it is resolved as **yes**. The machinery is already there (`rpcRequest`'s
`decode`), a rejection lands as `decode-error` — proof of life, never a contact strike — and the
cost is one pass over a small message. Without it a sender still reads `r.ok`, `.anchors`,
`.commitCertificate` off an unchecked cast.

- `sendPing` → `parsePingResponse`. This replaces today's `Boolean(r.ok)` coercion: a legal value
  always encodes as a boolean, so the coercion only ever hid a malformed peer.
- `sendMaybeAct` → `parseMaybeActReply` (discriminates `{ commitCertificate: string }` first, else
  NearAnchor).
- `fetchNeighbors` → the **same snapshot parser** as the announce handler, built from the same
  `mergeSnapshotCaps()`. Its signature must therefore take the parser (or the caps) rather than
  hard-coding one — the service is the only place that knows the profile.
- `announceNeighbors` and `sendLeave` are write-only (no `decode`); they gain nothing here.

### The merge-loop slices become dead — remove them

Once the parser truncates, the `slice(0, caps.*)` calls in `mergeAnnounceSnapshot`
(`fret-service.ts:1747`, `:1748`, `:1760`) and `fetchAndMergeSnapshot` (`:2237`, `:2238`, `:2249`)
are dead. **Remove them** and state in a `NOTE:` at `mergeSnapshotCaps` that the parser is now the
single enforcement point — leaving both is how two copies of a cap drift.

This is only safe because *both* merge paths are parser-covered after `15.32` + this ticket: the
announce path by the inbound handler's parser, the fetch path by `fetchNeighbors`' reply parser.
**If either half is missing, do not remove the slices** — say so in the handoff instead.

### Edge cases & interactions

- `fetchNeighbors`' parser now rejects snapshots the old cast accepted. That is a `decode-error`,
  which the service treats as proof of life — confirm no call site books a contact strike on it
  (`fetchAndMergeSnapshot`, `fret-service.ts:2213`, swallows its own errors and returns an empty
  snapshot; check the classification arms around `:2224`).
- A `sample` entry whose `coord` decodes to the wrong byte length must be dropped by the parser, not
  reach `upsert` — the store's write seam already throws on a non-32-byte coordinate.
- The `NearAnchor` caps (`anchors` ≤ 8, `cohort_hint` ≤ 16) are twice the largest lists our own
  producers emit, so the cap cannot refuse our own legal output. Confirm that against `pickAnchors`
  and the cohort-hint producer before trusting the numbers.

## Key tests to expect

Extend `test/rpc.codec-properties.spec.ts`:

- The round-trip property extended to the wired path: a legal reply produced by our own encoder
  survives its sender's `decode` — `rpcRequest` returns `ok`, never `decode-error`.
- A malformed reply (fuzzed field types) yields `decode-error`, never a throw out of the sender and
  never an `ok` carrying a half-parsed value.

Extend `test/rpc.handler-fuzz.spec.ts`:

- A snapshot carrying more ids than the profile cap, arriving on **both** merge paths (announce and
  fetch): assert the parse+hash+upsert loop runs exactly `caps.*` times, **by counting the loop's
  work** (a spy on the store's `upsert` / the hash) rather than by inferring it from the absence of
  a crash. This is the test that proves the removed slices were redundant.
