## Findings so far

### 1. The interleave assertion is vacuous (test defect, not a code defect)

In `churn.leave.spec.ts`, case `draws every replacement from beyond that window, both sides`, the
two closing assertions claim to pin that the replacement list is drawn from both sides of the ring:

```
expect(from([9, 10, 11, 12]).length,
    'interleaved, so the cap does not empty the clockwise side first').to.be.greaterThan(0)
expect(from([13, 14, 15, 16]).length,
    'interleaved, so the cap does not empty the counter-clockwise side first').to.be.greaterThan(0)
```

Both hold for **any** ordering, including the side-major ordering the change replaced, so they
discriminate nothing. Worked through by hand on the rig the case actually builds (24 peers at
offsets `+1..+24`, `m = 8`):

- The S/P window is `{+1..+8} ∪ {+17..+24}` — 16 of the 24 peers. What is left outside it is a
  **single arc of 8**, `{+9..+16}`.
- Both walks lap that ring, so *each* side, after exclusion, returns that same 8-id arc — the
  clockwise one as `+9…+16`, the counter-clockwise one as `+16…+9`. The candidate pool is 8 ids
  however the two are combined.
- `maxReplacements` is 6. Taking 6 of 8 leaves 2 behind, so each 4-id half of the arc must
  contribute at least 2 no matter what order the 8 are in. Both assertions are arithmetic, not
  behavior.
- Concretely, side-major ordering yields `[+10, +9, +11, +12, +13, +14]` after the
  connected-first sort — `from([13..16])` is `{13, 14}`, length 2, and the assertion passes.

A second, smaller reason the first of the two assertions cannot fail: `OUTSIDE_RECEIVER` is `+10`,
which is a real dialed node and therefore the *only* connected candidate in the pool.
`computeReplacements` sorts connected-first, so `+10` is first in the list under every ordering,
and `from([9, 10, 11, 12])` is non-empty for that reason alone.

**Disposition: fix in this review pass.** Two ways, pick one:

- *Preferred — make it discriminate.* Enlarge the ring to `COUNT = 40`. **Fully derived; the
  numbers below are checked, not sketched.** At 40 peers seeded at `+1..+40`, m = 8:
  - Window (`ids`, `count` 8/side, `reach` 9): clockwise `{+1..+8}`, counter-clockwise
    `{+40..+33}`. `spSet` = those 16.
  - Replacement walk (`count` 16/side, `exclude` 16, so `reach` 33 — and the ring is 40, so
    neither walk laps): clockwise raw `+1..+33`, minus `spSet` leaves `{+9..+32}`, trimmed to 16
    → `{+9..+24}`. Counter-clockwise raw `+40..+8`, minus `spSet` leaves `{+32..+9}`, trimmed to
    16 → `{+32..+17}`. The two arcs overlap on `+17..+24`; that is fine — `interleave` dedups
    through a `Set` (read this run in `src/service/ring-walk.ts`), and the overlap only appears
    past the 6-cap anyway. The discriminating region is `{+25..+32}`, which **only the
    counter-clockwise side can reach**.
  - Interleaved → `[9, 32, 10, 31, 11, 30, 12, 29, …]`; connected-first sort floats the one real
    dialed node to the head; the 6-cap yields three ids in `{+25..+32}`.
  - Side-major ordering (what the change replaced) yields `[9..24] ++ [32..25]`, connected-first
    → six ids from `{+9..+14}` after the cap — **nothing** in `{+25..+32}`. So an assertion
    "at least one replacement comes from `{+25..+32}`" fails under the old ordering and passes
    under the new one. That is the assertion the case is supposed to carry.
  - Knock-on edits in the same `describe`: `OUTSIDE` becomes `[9..32]` (the beyond-window set),
    `PRED_WINDOW` becomes `[40, 39, …, 33]`, `SUCC_WINDOW` is unchanged, `PRED_RECEIVER` becomes
    `40`.
  - **`OUTSIDE_RECEIVER` margin — refinement from run 3, do this.** Move it from `+10` to `+9`.
    `ids.length` is 16 at both `COUNT = 24` and `COUNT = 40` (m = 8 per side, ring ≥ 2m), so the
    beyond-S/P arm's ask is unchanged: `expandCohort(ids, selfCoord, fanOut, …)` for
    `ids.length + fanOut` = 20 ids, which the alternating walk fills roughly 10 per side — at
    `COUNT = 40` that is `{+1..+10} ∪ {+40..+31}`. `+10` therefore sits *exactly* at the clockwise
    edge of reach, one off-by-one from making `sends each peer exactly one notice` vacuous. `+9`
    is still outside the S/P window `{+1..+8}`, buys a slot of margin, and leaves the finding-1
    arithmetic intact (interleaved + connected-first → `[9, 32, 10, 31, 11, 30]`, three ids in
    `{+25..+32}`; side-major → `[9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]`, none). This buys margin; it does **not**
    retire the implicit test↔`expandCohort` coupling the implementer flagged — see *Remaining work*.
  - **Two things still to check before committing to this** (not done in any run):
    - `expandCohort`'s own source has **not** been read. The 20-id reach above is taken from the
      existing in-test comment plus the `ids.length` arithmetic, not from the function. Verify it.
    - `ghostPeerId()`'s cost at 40 seeded peers: 37 Ed25519 keygens where the rig does 21 today,
      inside a `before` hook with a 30 s timeout. A linear scale-up of a rig that currently
      passes, so low risk — but unmeasured.
- *Or* delete the two assertions and say plainly in the case's comment that the interleave itself is
  pinned by `test/ring-walk.spec.ts`, not here. Weaker, but honest; the rest of the case (all six
  replacements come from beyond the window) is real and does discriminate.

### 2. `computeReplacements`' comment overstates what the interleave buys

At `packages/fret/src/service/fret-service.ts` (in `computeReplacements`) the new comment ends:

> Interleaved, so the `maxReplacements` cap below takes from both sides rather than emptying the
> clockwise one first.

The cap is applied **after** a sort whose primary key is connected-first and whose secondary key is
relevance. The interleave therefore only decides order among candidates that tie on *both* — which
is the case in the specs (freshly `upsert`ed entries, relevance 0, none connected) but is not the
general case. On a live ring where connectedness and relevance differ, the cap takes the
best-connected and most-relevant six wherever they sit, and the interleave contributes nothing.

**Disposition: DONE (run 4).** The comment now states that the interleave is the tie-break the
sort falls back on, and that the cap takes the best-connected / most-relevant six wherever they
sit. The code was already correct; only the comment claimed more than it delivered.

### 3. Not defects — checked and clear

- **The `+ exclude.size` over-fetch is exact, not approximate.** `ringNeighborsBothSides` asks each
  side for `count + 1 + |exclude|`; `computeReplacements` passes an exclude of up to 2m = 16 against
  a count of `m * 2` = 16, so each side asks for 33 and can still yield 16 after every excluded id
  and self are dropped. A side the window blankets does not starve. (Re-read against
  `src/service/ring-walk.ts` this run: `reach = count + 1 + (exclude?.size ?? 0)`, `trimSide` drops
  then trims, `count <= 0` short-circuits to empty.)
- **The send loop's ordering is a real improvement under a truncated budget.** `ids` is interleaved
  (`s1, p1, s2, p2, …`), and the loop breaks on `budget.signal.aborted`, so a fan-out that runs out
  of the 3 s `SHUTDOWN_BUDGET_MS` has still reached both sides. Under the old side-major array it
  would have spent the whole budget clockwise. Worth stating in the findings section of the
  `complete/` ticket — it is a second, unclaimed benefit of the change.
- **No overlap between who is notified and who is advertised.** Everything in `spSet` is a notice
  target (modulo the `isDoomedDial` skip) and `spSet` is the replacement walk's `exclude`, so a peer
  cannot be both. That is exactly what case `advertises no peer that is inside its own S/P window`
  pins, and that case *does* discriminate — it fails under the old code.
- **The unfiltered target walk vs. the live-member replacement walk compose correctly.** `spSet` may
  hold `dead` / `foreign` ids that the filtered replacement walk would never have returned anyway;
  they cost one over-fetch slot each and change no result.

### 4. The `k: 7` spec's relabel *is* a tightening — the implementer's claim holds

Re-derived by hand, not taken at face value. Case `caps the replacement list at six ids`
(`k: 7`, m = 4, 20 seeded peers, real node at `+1`):

- Window: clockwise `{+1..+4}`, counter-clockwise `{+20..+17}`; `spSet` = 8 ids.
- Replacement walk: `count` 8/side, `exclude` 8, `reach` 17. Clockwise raw `+1..+17` minus `spSet`
  → `{+5..+16}`, trimmed to 8 → `[+5..+12]`. Counter-clockwise raw `+20..+4` minus `spSet` →
  `{+16..+5}`, trimmed to 8 → `[+16..+9]`.
- Interleaved → `[5, 16, 6, 15, 7, 14, 8, 13, 9, 12, 10, 11]`. No candidate is connected (`+1`, the
  only real node, is a notice target and therefore excluded), so the connected-first sort is inert
  and the stable sort preserves that order. The 6-cap yields `[+5, +16, +6, +15, +7, +14]` —
  **exactly** the `innermost` array the spec now asserts.
- Side-major ordering yields `[+5..+12] ++ [+16..+13]`, capped to `[+5, +6, +7, +8, +9, +10]`, which
  contains `+8`, `+9`, `+10` — none of them in `innermost`, so the case **fails** under the old
  ordering.

So unlike the `k: 15` case in finding 1, this one discriminates. The pool size is why: 12
candidates for 6 slots here, versus 8 for 6 at `k: 15`, where the cap can only ever drop 2 and no
6-of-8 selection can be side-pure.

**Disposition: nothing to do.** The source ticket's "the `k: 3` / `k: 7` specs must keep passing
unchanged" is arguably bent by the relabel, but the relabelled assertion is strictly stronger and
the old comment it replaced was factually wrong (it claimed an eight-id pool; it is twelve). Record
it in the `complete/` ticket as checked-and-accepted, not as a finding.

### What changed

One root cause, three readers, one array.

`sendLeaveToNeighbors` (`packages/fret/src/service/fret-service.ts`) built its neighbor list with a
side-major walk (all successors, then all predecessors) and capped the *concatenation* at 8. At the
shipped `k: 15` (so `m: 8`) those 8 slots were spent entirely on the clockwise side, so:

- no predecessor was ever sent a leave notice, and
- the same truncated array was handed to `computeReplacements` as "our own S/P window", so six
  genuine predecessors read as *outside* it and were advertised to our own neighbors as
  replacements for us.

Both symptoms are the one array. It is now a single `ringNeighborsBothSides(store, selfCoord, m,
selfStr)` call bound to one name, used unchanged by all three readers (send loop, replacement
exclusion, beyond-S/P fan-out filter). The `.slice(0, 8)` is gone — the fan-out was always
clock-bounded by `SHUTDOWN_BUDGET_MS` (3 s) overall and `LEAVE_NOTICE_TIMEOUT_MS` (1.5 s) per
notice, with the existing `budget.signal.aborted` break.

`computeReplacements` now uses the same helper at `m * 2` per side under `isLiveMember`, passing
`spNeighborIds` as the helper's `exclude` rather than filtering the walk's result. Its sort
(connected-first, then relevance) and its 6-cap are unchanged.

**Two intended behavior changes beyond the bug fix**, both worth a reviewer's eye:

1. **The beyond-S/P fan-out arm shrinks.** It filters on `!spSet.has(id)`, and `spSet` is now the
   true 2m window rather than one truncated side. Peers it used to reach now receive a notice as
   S/P members instead. Fewer sends there is the fix landing, not a regression — commented at the
   site.
2. **Replacement ordering is now interleaved, not side-major.** The helper returns
   `s1, p1, s2, p2, …`, so the 6-cap takes the innermost peers on *both* sides. The old union was
   side-major, so the cap filled from the clockwise side first and could empty it before reaching
   the counter-clockwise one. This is the helper's stated purpose ("a truncated result loses the
   outermost peers on both sides") and I take it as an improvement, but it *is* a wire-visible
   change to what a departing node advertises.

### Testing

New: `describe('at the shipped k of 15')` inside `Leave notice replacements (sender side)` in
`packages/fret/test/churn.leave.spec.ts` — four cases over one departure at `k: 15`, `m = 8`, 24
seeded live members:

- **notifies the predecessor side, not only the successors** — the headline. Real dialable nodes at
  `+1` and `+24`; both get exactly one notice. Under the old code `+24` got nothing.
- **advertises no peer that is inside its own S/P window** — `computeReplacements` ∩ `{+1..+8} ∪
  {+17..+24}` is empty.
- **draws every replacement from beyond that window, both sides** — all six come from `{+9..+16}`,
  with at least one from each side (pins the interleave; a side-major cap could satisfy the first
  half alone).
- **sends each peer exactly one notice, S/P and beyond-S/P together** — three real receivers, one
  notice each. Pins that the shared array cannot double-send or drop, which two divergent copies
  could.

The rig `makeSenderRig` was generalized to take a list of ring offsets that get real, dialable
nodes (was: exactly one, at `+1`) and to record *every* notice per receiver rather than the last
one. Existing callers pass no third argument and behave identically.
