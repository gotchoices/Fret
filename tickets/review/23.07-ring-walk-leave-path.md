## Findings — all closed, do not re-derive

### 1. The interleave assertion was vacuous — FIXED (run 6) and mutation-verified

Case `draws every replacement from beyond that window, both sides` closed with two assertions that
were true of *every* possible ordering: on the 24-peer rig the candidate pool was a single 8-id arc
and the cap was 6, so any 6-of-8 selection necessarily hit both halves.

Fixed by widening the rig to 40 peers, which separates the two sides' reach — the clockwise walk's
16-id trim stops at `+24`, so `{+25..+32}` is reachable **only** counter-clockwise — and asserting
at least one replacement comes from that arc. Verified by mutation: with `ring-walk.ts`'s
`interleave(successors, predecessors)` temporarily replaced by the side-major ordering this change
replaced, the case **fails**. `ring-walk.ts` was restored from backup and `git diff` on it is empty.

### 2. `computeReplacements`' comment overstated the interleave — FIXED (run 4)

The 6-cap is applied *after* a connected-first / relevance sort, so the interleave only decides
order among candidates tying on both keys. The comment now says that. The code was already correct.

### 5. The send-side comment claimed two readers in one place and three in another — FIXED (run 7)

The block opened `**One array, two readers.**` and then, forty lines later, said
"Third reader of `spSet`"; `docs/fret.md` says three. Corrected to three.

### 6. `matches handleLeave / computeReplacements` was wrong — FIXED (run 7)

The line justifying the unfiltered target walk pointed at two things that do not support it:
`handleLeave` performs no ring walk at all (it removes the departing peer and records replacement
hints), and `computeReplacements`' walk is `isLiveMember`-**filtered** — the opposite rule. This is
the unverified claim the previous run flagged. Replaced with the actual reason: the target list is
not advertised, it *defines* the window the replacement list excludes, and a `foreign`/`dead` id
there costs one `isDoomedDial` skip and nothing more.
