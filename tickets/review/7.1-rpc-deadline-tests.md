## What landed

**`packages/fret/test/deadline.spec.ts` (new, 10 tests)** — unit coverage of
`src/utils/deadline.ts`:

- `deadline` (7): fires after `ms` with a `DeadlineExpiredError` reason; `cancel()` before the
  budget leaves the signal un-aborted; parent abort propagates synchronously (asserted with no
  intervening `await`, because `openRpcStream` reads `signal.aborted` synchronously before
  dialing); `cancel()` after a parent abort is safe; an already-aborted parent yields an
  already-aborted child synchronously; double `cancel()` is a no-op; **and** `cancel()` detaches
  the parent listener so a later parent abort cannot reach the child (the `removeEventListener`
  half — nothing else pinned it).
- `abortReasonError` (3): an `Error` reason passes through by identity; a non-`Error` reason is
  wrapped as an `AbortError`; a missing reason names itself `aborted`.

**`packages/fret/test/rpc.protocols.spec.ts` (extended, +13 tests)**, on the existing stub-node
harness:

- `openRpcStream` signal forwarding (4): forwarded into `newStream` (connection path) and into
  `dialProtocol` (dial-fallback path), asserted by **identity** rather than presence; an
  already-aborted signal throws with the caller's reason and `dialProtocol` is never called; the
  same on the connection path, where `newStream` is never called either.
- `releaseRpcStream` (3): `close()` on the un-aborted path; `abort()` and **never** `close()` on
  the aborted path; a bare stream carrying neither method (and `undefined`) is swallowed.
- `RPC deadlines` (6) — the headline cases, all asserting **elapsed wall time**, not merely that
  the call settled:
  - `sendPing` against a `dialProtocol` that never resolves → rejects with `DeadlineExpiredError`
    in ≈100 ms.
  - `sendPing` against a stream that opens and never yields a chunk → rejects in ≈100 ms.
  - `sendPing` with an **already-aborted** caller `signal` → rejects with the caller's reason,
    never dials.
  - `sendPing` with a caller `signal` aborted **mid-flight** against a hanging dial → rejects
    with the caller's reason at ≈100 ms, against a 60 s own-budget (so only the parent can end it).
  - `readAllBounded` with a signal aborted mid-read against a silent iterable → rejects at
    ≈100 ms, against a 60 s read deadline (so the abort arm, not the deadline, is what ends it).
  - `fetchNeighbors` against a `newStream` that never resolves → returns its fabricated empty
    snapshot in ≈100 ms (it swallows every failure; see gap 2 below).

The hanging stubs settle **only** on signal abort — libp2p's own `AbortOptions` contract for
`dialProtocol` / `newStream` — and hang forever when handed no signal. That is what makes them
regression guards rather than descriptions.

## Known gaps — read these before treating the suite as a floor

1. **The silent-stream `sendPing` test does not guard signal threading.** It was one of the 14 that
   still *passed* during the mutation check: `readAllBounded` carries its own `timeoutMs` deadline,
   which bounds that path with or without the signal. What it does guard is that `sendPing`
   propagates its `timeoutMs` into the read at all — a real property, but a weaker one than the
   ticket implies.
2. **`fetchNeighbors` still cannot report a timeout.** Its test asserts on the fabricated empty
   snapshot plus elapsed time, because every failure — timeout and cancellation included — is
   swallowed into that snapshot. Pre-existing, tracked as `8-rpc-shared-helper`'s "fetchNeighbors
   fabricates success" arm; not in scope here, and the test is written so it will need rewriting
   when that lands.
3. **Three of the five senders have no deadline test at all**: `sendMaybeAct`, `sendLeave`,
   `announceNeighbors`. They share the `deadline()` + `openRpcStream` + `releaseRpcStream` shape
   with the two that are covered, so the argument for their correctness is structural, not
   asserted. A reviewer who wants per-sender coverage would add three near-identical hanging-dial
   cases; the stubs (`hangsUntilAbort`, `makeHangingDialNode`) are already factored for it.
4. **The cleared-timer half of `cancel()` has no handle probe.** Per the ticket's own instruction,
   the assertion is the suite passing the repo's mocha exit watchdog. The spec's direct assertion
   is only that the signal stays un-aborted 120 ms past a 30 ms budget.
5. **`readAllBounded`'s "a losing abort must never surface as an unhandled rejection" is covered by
   design, not by a test** — the abort arm resolves with a sentinel rather than rejecting, so there
   is nothing to leave unhandled. A test would assert the absence of a process crash, which is a
   weak assertion; it was deliberately not written.
6. **Three tests were added beyond the ticket's list** (the two caller-`signal` `sendPing` cases and
   the direct `readAllBounded` abort case). They cover the `stop()`-races-a-maintenance-tick path,
   which nothing else exercised. Flagged because they are scope the ticket did not ask for.

## Tripwires parked

- **Wall-clock flake risk**, parked as a `NOTE:` on the `describe('RPC deadlines')` doc comment in
  `test/rpc.protocols.spec.ts`. The six timing assertions are the only wall-clock-sensitive ones in
  the suite (100 ms budget checked against 80–1000 ms). If they flake on a loaded box, raise
  `MAX_MS` — and past ~1.5 s a `this.timeout()` is needed too, since mocha's 2 s per-test default
  is the harder ceiling. The `NOTE:` says explicitly not to swap the elapsed-time assertion for a
  bare "it rejected", since that is the assertion the original bug would have passed.

## Suggested review focus

- Are the hanging stubs an honest model of libp2p? They reject on abort and otherwise never
  settle. If real `dialProtocol` can resolve *after* an abort, or reject with something other than
  the abort reason, the timeout tests are optimistic.
- Gap 1 — is a test that passes without the fix worth keeping in the `RPC deadlines` block, or
  should it move somewhere that names what it actually guards?
- Gap 3 — is structural coverage of the other three senders acceptable, or should this ticket have
  produced a `debt-` follow-up for them?
