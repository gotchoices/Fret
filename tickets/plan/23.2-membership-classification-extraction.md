`membership-classification-strength` review named, and the largest of the three.

## What the candidate covers

Per `docs/fret.md`, *Ring membership (same-network vs foreign)* — the policy is already written
down in prose, which is a good sign for extractability. The pieces, as documented:

- The **evidence-strength guard**, `applyMembershipSignal(id, signal)` — the single choke point
  that encodes the ordering "a weaker or staler signal never overrides a stronger, more recent
  one", including the `unknown`-only rule for the negative identify arm.
- The **consecutive-negotiate-failure counter** and its 500 ms spacing rule (`negotiateFailures`,
  `lastNegotiateFailureAt` on the routing-table entry), demoting to `foreign` only at 3
  time-separated failures.
- The **inbound-RPC promotion** applied by all four inbound handlers from the
  transport-authenticated `connection.remotePeer`.
- The **identify-list classifier**, run from `peer:identify` / `peer:update` and from
  `seedFromPeerStore`.
- The **two bounded probe passes** — `classifyTargets` over `unknown` peers, and the two-armed
  `reprobeOffRingTargets` (foreign arm, dead arm) with their separate budgets and
  ascending-backoff-factor ordering.

## What the plan pass has to settle

Nothing here is decided yet. These are the questions, not the answers.

- **Is this one seam or several?** The guard, the counter, and the probe-target *selection* are
  plausibly one policy object over a narrow store interface. The probe *execution* is pooled RPC
  work bound to the tick budget and almost certainly stays in the service. Where the line falls
  determines whether this is one implement ticket or a chain.
- **What is the dependency actually on?** `isLiveMember` needed only a `PeerEntry`, which is why
  it lifted cleanly. Classification reads and writes `membership`, `negotiateFailures`,
  `lastNegotiateFailureAt`, `contactFailures`, and `state`, and the probe passes additionally read
  the per-label O(1) counts the store maintains at its write seam and the backoff map. Enumerate
  the exact surface before proposing an interface — a "narrow dependency on the store" is the
  claim to verify, not to assume.
- **Does the dead/liveness seam come with it, or stay?** The dead arm of the re-probe pass shares
  mechanics with the foreign arm but belongs to liveness, not membership; a successful ping there
  fixes both labels at once. Splitting them may be worse than leaving them together.
- **What does the extraction actually buy?** The stated cost today is that this policy is only
  reachable in tests through real libp2p nodes or private-member casts. Name the specific tests
  that become writable — that is the measure of whether the seam is in the right place.
- **Line count.** Measure the block with a command and record the number, the way the two sibling
  tickets do. "Largest of the three" is currently an impression, not a measurement.

## Constraints any design must respect

- **The store stays network-agnostic.** It stores and exposes `membership` and the counters and
  never branches on them; predicates are passed *in*. `docs/fret.md` states this as an invariant in
  two places — an extraction that teaches the store what a label means violates it.
- **One guard, stated once.** The whole point of `applyMembershipSignal` is that a call site added
  later inherits the evidence ordering rather than repeating the bug it exists to prevent. Any
  split that produces two places a label can be set is a regression regardless of line count.
- **The probe arms' budgets stay separate.** They are separate specifically so a large foreign
  population cannot starve dead recovery; merging the candidate lists repeals that rule.
- **`docs/fret.md` is the spec.** The *Ring membership* section is unusually complete; the design
  should be checkable against it line by line, and any divergence found while reading is a
  finding worth recording.
