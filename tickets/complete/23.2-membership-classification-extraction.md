Item (c) of the former `plan/23-fret-service-decomposition`. Items (a) and (b) landed as
`23-ring-neighbors-both-sides` and `23.1-size-observer-extraction`.

**Outcome: no seam. No implement ticket emitted.** The design question is settled and closed; the
decision is recorded at the code site so the next reader meets it there rather than re-deriving it.

## What was being asked

Whether the membership-classification policy — the evidence-strength ordering that decides when a
peer is labelled `member`, `foreign` or left `unknown` — could be lifted out of `FretService` into
its own module, and if so where the boundary should sit. Three candidate seams were on the table:
the guard alone, the guard plus off-ring probe-target selection, or nothing.

## The deciding measurement

An earlier run had established the size (~366 lines across two contiguous runs, the large majority
of it doc comment, in a file that is *shrinking* — 3299 lines, down from 3336) and the dependency
surface (the guard touches only the store, `Date.now()`, the protocol list and two class
constants). Neither settled the question: the case for extraction was always going to rest on
testability, so the deciding evidence was the test surface.

`packages/fret/test/ring-membership.spec.ts` carries 20 `as any` / `as unknown as` casts. They were
the stated cost of the policy being unreachable. Enumerated, they split:

- **18** reach probe-**backoff** internals — the private `backoffMap`, `recordBackoff`,
  `getBackoffPenalty`, `pruneBackoffMap` and the `BACKOFF_*` constants.
- **1** stubs `stabilizeOnce` to switch probing off.
- **1** — line 802 — reaches `applyMembershipSignal`, the guard this ticket was about.

So the seam under investigation accounts for one cast in twenty. The eighteen belong to a
different seam entirely, and are filed as `backlog/debt-backoff-map-test-surface`.

## Why the remaining tests do not move either

The `Ring membership classification` block (lines 511–889) is what makes this file expensive: nine
tests that each stand up two or three real libp2p nodes. They were the strongest-sounding argument
for extraction. Reading them, they need those nodes for the **wiring**, not for the policy:

- A genuine `UnsupportedProtocolError` produced by calling `svcC.stop()` — which unhandles the five
  namespaced protocols while leaving the node and connection up, the only faithful way to
  reproduce a restarting same-network peer.
- A genuine inbound dial on `/optimystic/net-a/fret/ping` from a node running no FRET service at
  all, which is the transport-authenticated promotion path.
- Genuine `busy` and undecodable replies written by a raw handler, exercising
  `probeMembership`'s two answered-badly arms.
- Two co-resident networks over one transport, which is the deployment the whole tri-state exists
  for.

A policy module extracted from `FretService` retires none of these. It would retire exactly one
test — `counts a burst of simultaneous negotiation failures as one observation` (line 796), which
is the only pure-policy test in the block. That test already builds a node solely to construct the
service, drives the guard directly through the one cast, and sleeps 1.2 s of real wall clock across
two `setTimeout(600)` calls to step outside the spacing window. It is genuinely the case an
injectable clock would improve — and it is one test.

## The decision, and its price

**Declined.** ~366 lines of mostly prose, one cast retired, one test made faster, against:

- The guard is *already* the single choke point the evidence-strength ordering needs. That
  property — "stated once, a call site added later inherits it" — is what `docs/fret.md` actually
  claims, and a module boundary does not strengthen it.
- Extraction separates `applyMembershipSignal`'s `negotiate-failure` arm from `applyContactStrike`,
  which is the same shape (spacing guard, clamped increment, threshold transition) with different
  constants and a different counter. There is an explicit `NOTE:` at `applyContactStrike` recording
  that the duplication was weighed and kept, with the revisit condition *"if a third spaced-run
  counter ever appears, factor all three out rather than adding another copy."* Moving one of the
  two instances behind a module boundary makes that condition unobservable — the next reader can no
  longer see the pair. Per the accepted-tradeoffs rule that decision stands and is not re-litigated
  here; it is simply a real cost of the extraction.
- Target selection (`classifyTargets` / `phaseTwoTargets` / the two re-probe arms) is worse still:
  it needs three injected predicates (`isConnected`, `hasAddresses`, `getBackoffPenalty`) plus the
  profile. That is a wide interface, and the peer-selection budgets it owns are already pinned by
  `test/stabilize-concurrency.spec.ts` without casts.

The price of declining is that the guard stays private and the one policy test keeps its cast and
its 1.2 s of sleeps. That is the accepted cost.

## What changed in the tree

One `NOTE:` added to the doc comment on `FretService.applyMembershipSignal`
(`packages/fret/src/service/fret-service.ts`), in the accepted-tradeoff form: what was declined,
why, and the revisit condition — *a third classification call site that cannot route through the
guard, or the policy growing state of its own beyond the store entry and the clock*. No behavior
changed; the edit is comment-only.

## Constraints checked while reading (all hold)

- **The store stays network-agnostic.** `digitree-store.ts` stores `membership`,
  `negotiateFailures` and `lastNegotiateFailureAt` and tallies per-label counts at its write seam,
  and never branches on what a label means. Unchanged.
- **One guard, stated once.** Unchanged — declining the extraction is what preserves it.
- **The probe arms' budgets stay separate.** Unchanged; no candidate lists merged.
- **`docs/fret.md` matches the code.** The *Ring membership* section's evidence-strength table
  matches the `MembershipSignal` union and the guard's five arms one for one, including the
  round-trip-not-reply-body rule and the `noteAnsweredOnProtocol` / `applySuccess` split. No
  divergence found, so no doc edit was needed.

## Review findings

- The measured test-surface cost of policy-in-the-service lands on the **probe-backoff** seam
  (18 of 20 casts), not on the membership guard (1 of 20). Filed as
  `tickets/backlog/debt-backoff-map-test-surface.md`.
- The extraction was weighed and declined; recorded as an accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` at
  `FretService.applyMembershipSignal` rather than left only in this archive, so the next reviewer
  meets the decision at the site.
