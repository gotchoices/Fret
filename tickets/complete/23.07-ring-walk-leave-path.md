## What shipped

The graceful-departure path now derives its notice targets from a single two-sided ring walk
(`ringNeighborsBothSides` at `m` per side, 2m = 16 ids at the shipped k of 15) instead of a
`.slice(0, 8)` over a side-major concatenation, which at m = 8 made the list successors-only — the
predecessor side of a departing peer received no notice at all. That same array is the one the
replacement walk excludes and the one the step-3 "notify peers outside S/P" fan-out filters
against, so the three readers can no longer disagree; under the old cap, six genuine predecessors
read as *outside* the departing peer's window and were advertised to its own neighbors as
replacements for it.

The target cap is gone entirely; the fan-out is bounded by the clock instead
(`SHUTDOWN_BUDGET_MS` 3 s over the whole fan-out, `LEAVE_NOTICE_TIMEOUT_MS` 1.5 s per notice),
which is what the cap was standing in for and never actually did. Replacements stay
live-member-scoped, so a peer already marked `foreign` or `dead` is never advertised.

`docs/fret.md`'s *Leave* section documents the new behavior.

## Review findings

- **Test defect found and fixed in this pass.** The `k: 15` interleave case
  (`draws every replacement from beyond that window, both sides`) closed with two assertions that
  were true of *every* possible ordering: on the 24-peer rig the candidate pool was a single 8-id
  arc and the cap was 6, so any 6-of-8 selection necessarily hit both halves — the case
  discriminated nothing. Rig widened 24 → 40 peers, which separates the two sides' reach (the
  clockwise walk's 16-id trim stops at `+24`, so `{+25..+32}` is reachable only counter-clockwise),
  and the case now asserts at least one replacement comes from that arc. Verified by mutation:
  with `interleave(successors, predecessors)` temporarily replaced by the side-major ordering this
  change replaced, the case fails. `ring-walk.ts` was restored afterwards; its `git diff` is empty.
- **Three comment defects found and fixed in this pass.** No production behavior was changed by
  the review.
  - `computeReplacements`' comment overstated what the interleave buys: the 6-cap is applied
    *after* a connected-first / relevance sort, so the interleave only decides order among
    candidates tying on both keys. The code was already correct.
  - `sendLeaveToNeighbors`' comment block opened `**One array, two readers.**` and then, forty
    lines later, said "Third reader of `spSet`"; `docs/fret.md` says three. Corrected to three.
  - The line justifying the *unfiltered* target walk pointed at "matches handleLeave /
    computeReplacements", and neither supports it — `handleLeave` performs no ring walk at all
    (it removes the departing peer and records replacement hints), and `computeReplacements`' walk
    is `isLiveMember`-**filtered**, the opposite rule. Replaced with the actual reason: the target
    list is not advertised, it *defines* the window the replacement list excludes, and a
    `foreign`/`dead` id there costs one `isDoomedDial` skip and nothing more.
- **Tripwires parked, not filed as tickets.** The implementer's serial-send `NOTE:` at
  `sendLeaveToNeighbors` (notices go out serially; pool through `runPooled` like the stabilization
  tick *if* departure healing latency ever matters) — kept as-is. And a `NOTE:` added at
  `OUTSIDE_RECEIVER` in `test/churn.leave.spec.ts`, recording that the offset assumes
  `expandCohort`'s fan-out reach rather than asserting it, so a future change to `fanOut` would
  make that case vacuous rather than fail.
- **New tickets filed: none.** Every finding resolved at its own site in this pass; nothing rose to
  a class needing an invariant, and no finding was a one-off defect left standing.
- **Considered and declined.** `sendLeaveToNeighbors`' comment block length — ~20 lines of comment
  above one line of code, part of it a bug post-mortem also in `docs/fret.md`; house style here is
  heavily commented and duplicating the post-mortem at the site is the house pattern, and the real
  defect was its two false statements, now fixed. And `registerLeave`'s unread `{ok: true}` reply,
  whose accepted-tradeoff `NOTE:` names a revisit condition (`sendLeave` reading a reply) that has
  not tripped.
- **Checked and clear, with reasons.**
  - The `+ exclude.size` over-fetch is exact, not approximate: each side asks for
    `count + 1 + |exclude|` = 33 against a count of 16, so a side the window blankets still
    yields 16.
  - No overlap between who is notified and who is advertised — `spSet` is both the notice target
    set and the replacement walk's `exclude`.
  - The unfiltered target walk and the live-member replacement walk compose correctly: `spSet` may
    hold `dead`/`foreign` ids the filtered walk would never return; each costs one over-fetch slot
    and changes no result.
  - The `k: 7` spec's relabel is a strict tightening, re-derived by hand and confirmed by mutation
    (under side-major ordering that case fails). The comment it replaced was factually wrong — it
    claimed an 8-id pool; it is 12.
  - `src/rpc/leave.ts` (72 lines) is clean and untouched by this change.
  - `test/ring-walk.spec.ts` (349 lines) closes both gaps the implementer left open, which is why
    no extra cases were added to the leave spec: the single-peer ring is pinned directly (`a ring
    of exactly one peer, which is self, returns empty`), making the leave send loop a
    for-over-empty-array, and walk ordering is pinned by the fixture's ring-order oracle, which
    asserts *which* peers come back and in what order rather than only how many.
  - `docs/fret.md` is accurate beyond the two hunks the commit edited. The *Leave* section's
    per-leave outbound ceiling describes the **receive**-side cost (0 pings, ≤ 12 hash + upsert,
    one debounced announce) and is unaffected by the wider **send**-side target list, which step 1
    already documents as 2m = 16. `### Configuration` (k 15, m = ceil(k/2) = 8) is consistent with
    it, and there is no leave-specific knob to keep in sync.
- **A second, unclaimed benefit of the change** worth recording: `ids` is interleaved and the send
  loop breaks on `budget.signal.aborted`, so a leave fan-out truncated by `SHUTDOWN_BUDGET_MS` has
  still reached both sides of the ring; the old side-major array spent the whole budget clockwise.
