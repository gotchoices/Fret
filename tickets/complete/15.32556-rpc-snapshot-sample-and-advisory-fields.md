### Minor — fixed in this pass

- **The over-cap sample row did not pin slice-then-filter order, though the handoff claimed it
  did.** Its 10 entries were all valid, so slice-then-filter and filter-then-slice produce the
  identical first 8 — the row could not fail if the two were swapped. The id-list matrix has a
  dedicated order-pinning row (`truncateThenFilter`); `sample` had no analogue. Added
  `sample: truncate-then-filter order (valid entry past the 8 slice)` — 8 unusable entries then
  one valid entry, expecting `[]`. Under filter-then-slice an over-long list of junk could smuggle
  real entries in behind the cap; that is now a failing case.
- **Sample entries are rebuilt, not passed through — untested.** `parseSample` constructs
  `{ id, coord, relevance }` from the three vetted fields, so extra keys on a received entry never
  reach the merge loop. That differs from `sig` at the top level (deliberately carried through) and
  was unpinned. Added a row asserting extras are dropped.
- **Only one of the two coord throw sites was covered.** The wrong-width rows trip
  `base64urlToCoord`'s length check; nothing tripped the base64url decoder underneath it. Added
  rows for an undecodable coord and an empty-string coord. Both must be caught inside
  `parseSample`, or a malformed coord escapes to the store's write seam inside a merge loop —
  exactly the throw-in-the-loop leak the parser module exists to prevent.
