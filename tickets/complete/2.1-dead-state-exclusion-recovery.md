Second half of the dead-state work (Phases 4–6), reviewed and landed. The sibling
`dead-state-liveness-seam` ticket added the contact-failure counter, the `dead` transition and the
recovery helper; this one makes the label *mean* something (exclusion from routing) and makes it
reversible without outside help (a dead re-probe arm).

### What shipped

**Exclusion is one predicate.** `membership === 'member' && state !== 'dead'` is the predicate every
ring-shaped read funnels through, so the successor/predecessor windows, cohort assembly, the
routing-candidate walk, the size estimate, the outgoing snapshot's neighbor lists and sparsity
sample, and capacity protection all inherit both exclusions at once. Two things fall out with no
code of their own: FRET stores no separate successor/predecessor *set*, so exclusion from the
predicate **is** the "remove from S/P" the design asks for; and a dead peer loses capacity
protection, so with a decayed relevance it sorts to the front of the eviction victim list.

**Maintenance fan-outs that walk the store unfiltered** (announce targets, leave notices) carry the
skip explicitly, because an unfiltered walk is what keeps a freshly-connected `unknown` peer from
stalling bootstrap.

**Recovery pass.** `reprobeExcludedPeers` runs two arms — `foreign` and `dead` — over shared
mechanics (`reprobeOffRing`: off-backoff and reachable candidates, ascending backoff factor first, a
per-tick budget of Core 2 / Edge 1 each, one namespaced ping). Separate budgets, not one merged
list, so a large foreign population cannot starve dead recovery. The arms are disjoint: a
foreign+dead peer belongs to the dead arm alone and one successful ping fixes both labels.

**Docs** (`docs/fret.md`) updated across *Stabilization and churn handling*, *Leave*, the re-probe
bullet under *Ring membership*, and *Network-scoped admission*.

### Review findings

Read the implement diff (`e6fdf69`) before the handoff summary, then the surrounding call sites.
Everything below was checked; the empty categories say why they are empty.

**Fixed in this pass (minor)**

- **Leave notices advertised peers we had given up on.** `computeReplacements` walked the store
  *unfiltered*, so the suggested-replacement list a departing node puts on the wire could name
  `dead` and `foreign` peers. That is the same transitive-propagation channel the outgoing
  snapshot's neighbor lists and sample are scoped for, and the recipient acts on it directly —
  `handleLeave` dials and pings up to six of the ids — so the list was spending someone else's dial
  budget on peers we had already excluded, and re-seeding a foreign peer into a same-network view.
  Missed by the membership ticket for `foreign` and by this one for `dead`. The walk now filters on
  `isLiveMember`; the notice's *targets* stay unfiltered, since that walk defines the S/P set the
  replacement list excludes rather than being a list we advertise. New test:
  *"never advertises a dead or foreign peer as a leave-notice replacement"*. Doc'd in *Leave*.
- **The "one predicate" claim was a convention, not a fact.** `FretPeerDiscovery` is a ring-shaped
  reader living outside `FretService`, and it held its *own* copy of the two conditions
  (`peer-discovery.ts:118,122`) while `isLiveMember` was module-private to `fret-service.ts`. Two
  copies of an invariant whose whole stated value is that "a reader added later cannot forget one".
  Extracted to `src/service/live-member.ts` and imported by both. The implement handoff had noticed
  the duplicate and chose to leave it; this is the architecture-first version of that call — the
  reason the copy was harmless is exactly the reason it should not exist.
- **The two doomed-dial guards had drifted.** Announce skipped undialable + `foreign` + `dead`; the
  leave path skipped undialable + `dead` only, so a leave notice still dialed a confirmed-foreign
  neighbor inside `stop()` for a guaranteed `UnsupportedProtocolError` — the exact cost the guard
  exists to avoid. Unified into one named predicate, `isDoomedDial`. This also closes the
  implementer's admitted "the leave-path dead skip has no test" gap: the classification is now a
  pure predicate with its own test (*"treats undialable, foreign, and dead maintenance targets as
  doomed dials"*, including the `unknown`-is-not-doomed arm), and the announce path still drives it
  end to end through the choke point. A redundant `isDoomedDial` on the leave fan-out's `extra` list
  was deliberately *not* added — those ids come from the live-member-scoped `assembleCohort` and are
  already `isConnected` — with a comment saying so.

**Tripwires recorded (not tickets)**

- **Shared backoff across the two re-probe arms.** `reprobeOffRing` reads one `backoffMap`, so a
  peer that accumulated backoff while `foreign` carries it into the dead arm. Intended — same
  "don't hammer this peer" budget, and the arms are disjoint so no peer is charged twice per tick.

  arms needs separate maps, not just separate budgets).
- Three full-store walks per tick was already recorded by the implementer as a `NOTE:` at
  `classifyUnknownPeers`; left there, and cross-referenced from ticket 15 above.

**Checked and found clean**

- **The dead arm's reachability filter.** A dead peer that is neither connected nor has a peerStore
  address is never a re-probe candidate. Not a defect: a bare-id dial to it can only raise
  `NoValidAddressesError`, so there is nothing to probe with. Its ways back remain an inbound RPC, a
  fresh `peer:connect`, or eviction at capacity (after which it re-enters as `unknown`).
- **`enforceCapacity` interaction.** Eviction of a dead peer is self-healing rather than a black
  hole — a re-seed reinserts it as `unknown`/`disconnected` and the classification pass takes it.
- **`handleLeave`'s `base` walk** stays unfiltered, and correctly so: it is the "already known" set
  subtracted from the live-member-scoped `expandCohort` result, so dead peers cannot leak into
  `localNew` through it.
- **`applyContactStrike` / `noteProofOfLife` / `noteDisconnected`** re-read against the new
  exclusion: self-guard, spacing guard, clamp, and the "a disconnect is not proof of life" asymmetry
  all still hold with `dead` now load-bearing for routing.
- **The five announce call sites** all funnel through the one choke point, so the guard is not
  restated per site.

**No major findings.** Not "looks good" — the exclusion is genuinely a one-line predicate change at
a seam that already existed, and the recovery arm reuses mechanics the foreign arm had already
proven. The three fixes above are all at the *unfiltered* maintenance walks, which is precisely
where the design says the predicate cannot reach, so that is where the gaps were.

**No new blocked/backlog tickets.** Nothing here needed a human decision or waited on anything
outside the repo.

**Accepted tradeoffs left alone.** The `NOTE:`-tagged decisions at `enforceCapacity` (list-and-sort

revisit condition tripped by this change.

### Known gaps carried forward, honestly

- The **resurrection-race test asserts an invariant** (`dead` ⟺ counter at threshold), not a
  schedule. Pinning "the last writer wins outright" needs a deterministic scheduler, not a
  `Promise.all`. Left as the implementer wrote it — the invariant is the property that matters.
- The **announce-skip test stubs the token bucket** to count attempts, because a dial to a synthetic
  peer id can never succeed. It proves guard ordering but touches a private field.
- The **size-estimate assertion is directional** (`after < before`), not a pinned value.
