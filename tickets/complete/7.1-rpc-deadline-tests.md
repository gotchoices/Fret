## What landed

**`packages/fret/test/deadline.spec.ts` (new, 10 tests)** — unit coverage of `src/utils/deadline.ts`:

- `deadline` (7): fires after `ms` with a `DeadlineExpiredError`; `cancel()` before the budget
  leaves the signal un-aborted; parent abort propagates synchronously (asserted with no intervening
  `await`, because `openRpcStream` reads `signal.aborted` synchronously before dialing); `cancel()`
  after a parent abort is safe; an already-aborted parent yields an already-aborted child
  synchronously; double `cancel()` is a no-op; `cancel()` detaches the parent listener.
- `abortReasonError` (3): an `Error` reason passes through by identity; a non-`Error` reason is
  wrapped as an `AbortError`; a missing reason names itself `aborted`.

**`packages/fret/test/rpc.protocols.spec.ts` (extended, +16 tests)** on the existing stub-node harness:

- `openRpcStream` signal forwarding (4): forwarded into `newStream` and into `dialProtocol`,
  asserted by **identity** rather than presence; an already-aborted signal throws with the caller's
  reason and neither path opens anything.
- `releaseRpcStream` (3): `close()` on the un-aborted path; `abort()` and never `close()` on the
  aborted path; a stream carrying neither method (and `undefined`) is swallowed.
- `RPC deadlines` (9) — all asserting **elapsed wall time**, not merely that the call settled:
  `sendPing` against a hanging dial, against a stream that opens and goes silent, with an
  already-aborted caller signal, and with a caller signal aborted mid-flight against a 60 s own
  budget; `readAllBounded` aborted mid-read against a 60 s read deadline; `fetchNeighbors` against a
  hanging `newStream`; and — **added by this review** — `sendMaybeAct`, `sendLeave` and
  `announceNeighbors` against a hanging dial.

The hanging stubs settle **only** on signal abort, which is libp2p's own `AbortOptions` contract for
`dialProtocol` / `newStream`, and hang forever given no signal.

## Review findings

**Checked:** the full implement diff read before the handoff summary; `src/utils/deadline.ts` and
`src/rpc/protocols.ts` line by line against the tests that claim to cover them; all five senders
(`ping`, `neighbors` × 2, `maybe-act`, `leave`) for whether the "they share the same shape" argument
in the handoff's gap 3 actually holds; libp2p's `@libp2p/interface` stream typings for what each
awaited call does and does not accept; `docs/fret.md` for staleness against the new whole-RPC
budget; the ticket board (`grep -rl` over every open stage) for prior claims on these files; test
hygiene (wall-clock slack vs mocha's 2 s default, stub honesty, handle cleanup, unhandled
rejections); lint (none exists in this repo — `yarn check` is the gate, and `yarn format` is
documented as not runnable here) and the full test suite.

**Major — one finding, filed as an arm on the ticket that already owns the site.**
`close()` is the one await in an outbound RPC that the deadline does not cover. libp2p's
`Stream.close(options?: AbortOptions)` takes a cancellation signal and resolves only once unsent
data has been flushed, but neither `releaseRpcStream`'s un-aborted arm (`protocols.ts:317`) nor
`sendMaybeAct`'s half-close (`maybe-act.ts:64`) passes one — so a peer that accepts a stream and
stops reading can hold a call past its whole-RPC budget, worst on `stop()`'s leave fan-out where the
budget exists to bound shutdown. Established statically; no magnitude claimed for the tail, because
no default for the muxer's `inactivityTimeout` backstop exists in the installed dependency tree.
Climbing the architecture ladder puts the durable fix at rung 1: the shared `rpcRequest` helper
owning every await makes an unsignalled one unrepresentable, and `tickets/plan/8-rpc-shared-helper`
already owns `src/rpc/*` and the "an AbortSignal bounds every RPC" requirement — so this went there
as the "close() escapes the deadline" arm rather than becoming a second point ticket. Not fixed in
this pass: it is a source change, and this ticket's mutation checks depend on `src/` staying
untouched.

**Minor — fixed inline.**
- Gap 3 of the handoff closed: `sendMaybeAct`, `sendLeave` and `announceNeighbors` had no deadline
  test and were argued correct structurally. That argument was wrong on its own terms — those three
  also `close()` the stream, which is exactly the step that escapes the budget (the major finding
  above), so "same shape as the two covered senders" was not true. Three hanging-dial tests added,
  mutation-checked, with a comment at the block stating what is and is not common between them.
- The parked wall-clock tripwire claimed its six assertions were "the only wall-clock-sensitive
  assertions in the suite"; `deadline.spec.ts` carries one more (a lower bound on a 50 ms budget).
  NOTE reworded to scope the claim to its own file and point at the other.

`test/rpc.protocols.spec.ts` (raise `MAX_MS` if it flakes; past ~1.5 s a `this.timeout()` is needed
too, since mocha's 2 s default is the harder ceiling; do not weaken the assertion to a bare "it
rejected"). Re-read and confirmed accurate apart from the scope overclaim fixed above. No new
tripwires: nothing else here is conditional — the one remaining concern is a live defect, ticketed.

**Considered and declined — no new ticket.**

  `readAllBounded` carries its own deadline) was offered for relocation. Left where it is: it
  asserts that `sendPing` propagates its budget into the read, which is a genuine part of the
