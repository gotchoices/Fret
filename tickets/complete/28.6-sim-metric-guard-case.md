## What the implementation added

A fourth case in `packages/fret/test/simulation.routing.spec.ts` (+96 lines) that bounds every
simulated peer's routing table to 32 entries — 3.2% of a 1000-peer ring — and asserts both a
success-rate floor (95%) and a p90 hop-count ceiling (12) over 100 routes. This is the first
simulation case that can tell the shipped ring distance metric from a plausible wrong one; the
three pre-existing cases leave stores unbounded, where any roughly-monotone metric routes in one
or two hops. Three supporting comment-only edits (`next-hop.ts`, `fret-sim.ts`, `docs/fret.md`)
retract an earlier claim that the spec could not discriminate metrics at all.

## Review findings

### Verified — the guard actually bites (measured, not inferred)

Ran the capacity-bounded case alone against three substituted bodies of `minDistance` in
`src/ring/distance.ts`, roughly 6 seconds per run:

| `minDistance` body | success | p90 | max | case outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (baseline)  | 100% | 8  | 19 | passes |
| clockwise-only      | 83%  | 18 | 23 | **fails** |
| XOR                 | 71%  | 6  | 9  | **fails** |

Baseline reproduces the handoff's numbers exactly (100% success, p90 8, max 19, knowledge 3.2%),
and each substituted row reproduces its cell in the spec's own table.

- **Both assertions are load-bearing, which is the specific thing this ticket existed to confirm
  rather than assume.** XOR fails the success floor while sailing past the hop bound — its p90 of
  6 is half the ceiling of 12, because p90 is taken over successful routes only and XOR simply
  fails the hard routes, so its survivors flatter it. Clockwise-only fails the other way. Neither
  assertion alone catches both wrong metrics.
- One correction to the previous review ticket's expectation, in the code's favour: under
  clockwise-only *both* assertions would fail (p90 18 exceeds 12), but `successRate` is asserted
  first in the file so it trips first. The spec's own inline comments already describe it that
  way, so the ticket's prediction was wrong, not the code's.
- A fifth, pre-existing case (`an originator already nearest the key has no strictly-improving
  first hop`) also fails under a substituted metric. That is correct behaviour rather than
  collateral damage — it cross-checks the shipped metric against an independent bigint
  reimplementation in `test/helpers/ring.ts`, which is exactly what its doc-block says it is for.
  Not filed.
- `src/ring/distance.ts` was restored after each run; the working tree carries no substitution.

### Fixed in this pass (minor)

One comment overstated its evidence. The line above the `knowledgeFraction < 0.05` assertion read
"Every store saturates at the cap, so knowledge is capacity/n = 3.2% **by construction**". The cap
is genuinely a ceiling, and three independent runs each printed exactly 3.2%, but saturation was
never read off the harness — no code path guarantees every peer learns 32 distinct peers. Rewrote
the comment to separate the ceiling (which the assertion is actually written against) from the
observed saturation (which is evidence, not a proof), and to note that a run failing to saturate
would still pass the assertion. Comment-only; the assertion itself is unchanged and correct.

### Considered and kept

`expect(r.p90Hops, ...).to.be.at.least(3)` — the implementer flagged this as a headroom claim
rather than a guard and the previous ticket invited deleting it. Kept. It is a premise sentinel,
the hop-count analogue of the `knowledgeFraction` bound directly above it, and it is not a
duplicate of that bound: knowledge pins how much of the ring each peer stores, while a future
change to gossip or to the selector could hold knowledge at 3.2% and still collapse routes to one
or two hops — at which point the case silently stops discriminating metrics and every assertion
in it passes vacuously. Failing loudly is the right outcome there. The margin is real (shipped
p90 measures 7–8 across four seeds, against a floor of 3), so it is not fragile at today's
numbers. Its existing comment already states the role accurately and was left alone.

### Documentation — checked, accurate

Read the *Testing strategy* section of `docs/fret.md` in full against all three comment-only
source edits. All three carry the same retraction and agree with each other and with the measured
table: none still claims a substituted metric moves neither number, and all three state the same
bound on what the case proves — it discriminates the distance *metric*, not the next-hop cost
function's slack constants, because the simulator's near radius (`beta * k * 2^256 / store.size()`)
has the store size as its own denominator and so clamps to half the ring at 32 entries, putting
every candidate on the selector's near branch where the slack constants are inert. That arithmetic
was independently re-derived and is correct. No documentation drift found; nothing to update.

### Known gaps — carried forward as stated limits, not filed

The implementer flagged these and they remain accurate; each is already recorded at its own code
site, so none is a new ticket:

- The metric substitution is not a pure selector comparison. There is no injection seam, so
  `minDistance` was edited in place, and `normalizedLogDistance` in `src/store/relevance.ts` reads
  it too — every substituted run therefore changes eviction shape as well as hop choice. It shows
  a wrong metric is detectable; it does not isolate the selector.
- The selector's far-cost branch has no simulation coverage, for the near-radius reason above.
  Exercising it needs a larger capacity or a smaller beta, not a sparser store.
- `max` hops carries no assertion: shipped 19 against clockwise-only 23 is too narrow a gap to
  hold a threshold.
- Thresholds were taken from the prerequisite ticket's four-seed table rather than independently
  re-measured, though the cap-32 row was reproduced exactly here.
